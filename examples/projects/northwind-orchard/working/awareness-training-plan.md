1. **Confirm audience and governance before assigning training.** Ask the client to validate workforce size, roles, contractors, relevant suppliers, languages, accessibility needs, approved scope, and the person authorized to approve content. Keep the intake identity and all reported details marked unverified until corroborated.
2. **Set common foundational learning objectives.** Propose a short core module for the approved audience covering: protecting information and devices; recognizing suspicious messages and social engineering; safe use of identity and authentication; prompt reporting of suspected incidents; and safe remote work. Have the client confirm examples and reporting routes before use. Do not present any reporting channel or control as currently implemented unless evidence is supplied.
3. **Add role-based modules only after role validation.** If confirmed in scope, prepare focused learning for developers and deployment staff on handling secrets and sensitive data, reviewing changes, and reporting suspected exposure; for administrators on privileged access and secure operational changes; and for support or business staff on identity verification, information sharing, and escalation. These are proposed topics, not statements about current practices or failures.
4. **Use safe, discussion-based practice.** Offer scenario walkthroughs using fictional messages and synthetic data: a suspicious email, an unexpected authentication prompt, a misplaced device, or a suspected data disclosure.
